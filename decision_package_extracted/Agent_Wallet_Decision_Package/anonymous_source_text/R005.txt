L02044 ### 1.1 Core Findings
L02045 This report presents an independent investigation into the optimal solution for Topic F (Agent Wallet) of the 2026 Shenzhen International FinTech Competition. After verifying official requirements, surveying global prior art across 50+ products and research projects, cataloging 20 real-world financial problems, generating 20 differentiated solution concepts, and conducting rigorous threat modeling, the following key findings emerge:
L02046 **Finding 1: The Competition's Real Differentiator is Security Architecture, Not AI Capability.**
L02047 The 40% task completion / 30% security / 30% innovation split means that security-conscious designs are weighted equally with innovation. The competition explicitly requires a "Security self-assessment report" and "real on-chain transaction hashes" as execution evidence. This creates a clear strategic opportunity: teams that architect robust, independently-enforceable security boundaries — rather than relying on prompt engineering — will have a structural advantage.
L02048 **Finding 2: A Critical Architectural Gap Exists in the Market.**
L02049 Existing agent wallet infrastructure (Coinbase Agentic Wallets, Privy, Crossmint, Turnkey, Circle Agent Stack, Kite Passport, Skyfire) almost universally separates the agent's *planning* from the wallet's *policy enforcement*. However, nearly all of these rely on off-chain policy engines or server-side guardrails. Only Safe Smart Accounts with on-chain modules (Zodiac Roles Modifier, Allowance Module) provide truly on-chain, independently verifiable policy enforcement. This gap represents both a competitive differentiator and a genuine security improvement opportunity.
L02050 **Finding 3: The Most Compelling Competition Strategy Combines On-Chain Policy Enforcement with Verifiable Agent Reasoning.**
L02051 The strongest concept identified is a **"Policy-Gated Agent Wallet with On-Chain Proof of Intent"** — a system where the AI agent's proposed transactions are validated against on-chain policies (session keys with ERC-4337/7579 modules) *and* the agent's decision chain is cryptographically committed to an audit trail. This directly addresses the official scoring criteria: high task completion (working end-to-end execution), strong security (on-chain enforceable boundaries), and genuine innovation (verifiable agent intent).
L02052 **Finding 4: The "0-5 Point Challenge Bonus" is Most Likely to Reward Real On-Chain Complexity.**
L02053 The bonus is described as "on-chain exploration challenge bonus." It is likely to reward teams that demonstrate genuine on-chain sophistication — not just testnet transactions, but meaningful smart contract interaction, multi-step on-chain workflows, or novel on-chain primitives. A simple ERC-20 transfer will almost certainly not earn the full bonus.
L02054 **Finding 5: The Safest Implementable Approach is to Build on Proven Infrastructure (Safe + ERC-4337/7579 + Privy/Crossmint for key management) Rather than Custom Smart Contracts.**
L02055 Given the student team context and the competition timeline (submission deadline October 20, 2026), building on battle-tested infrastructure is critical. Safe Smart Accounts are the most widely deployed and audited smart account infrastructure. ERC-4337 and ERC-7579 provide standardized session key and module frameworks. Privy or Crossmint can handle key management without exposing private keys to the agent.
L02056 **Finding 6: The Most Original but Higher-Risk Concept is a Fully On-Chain "Agent Treasury" with Programmable Obligations.**
L02057 This concept would implement a DAO/SME treasury where the AI agent manages cash flow, executes payments, and enforces financial obligations entirely through on-chain smart contracts with no off-chain policy engine. While technically ambitious, it demonstrates the deepest on-chain exploration and would likely earn the maximum challenge bonus — but carries significant implementation risk.
L02058 **Finding 7: The Idea That Looks Impressive but Should Be Avoided is a Generic "AI Trading Bot" for DeFi Yield Optimization.**
L02059 This is the most obvious interpretation of "agent wallet" and is already saturated with competitors (Coinbase, MetaMask, countless hackathon projects). It would score poorly on innovation (30%) and would face intense scrutiny on security, as DeFi trading introduces MEV, slippage, oracle manipulation, and smart contract risks. The competition explicitly states that "the on-chain topic does not affect scoring" and warns against "technological spectacle."

L03202 ## 18. CONCLUSION
L03203 ### The Strongest Evidence-Backed Idea
L03204 **PolicyGuard — AI Agent Wallet with On-Chain Policy Enforcement.** This concept has the strongest evidence base: it builds on Safe Smart Accounts (the most audited smart account infrastructure), uses standardized ERC-4337 and ERC-7579 session key mechanisms, and implements policy enforcement at the smart contract level where it cannot be bypassed by a compromised LLM. The security architecture directly addresses the documented insufficiency of prompt-based defenses. Feasibility is high for a student team with a 2-4 week timeline.
L03205 ### The Most Original But Higher-Risk Idea
L03206 **IntentLedger — Verifiable Intent with On-Chain Proof.** This concept introduces genuine novelty by cryptographically committing the agent's decision rationale to an on-chain audit trail before execution. It directly addresses the "verifiable intent" problem identified in academic research (Mastercard/Google Verifiable Intent, HJS). However, the complexity of intent representation and the risk of over-engineering make it higher-risk for a competition MVP.
L03207 ### The Safest Implementable Idea
L03208 **EmergencyStop — Autonomous Wallet Incident Response.** This concept has a clear, easily demonstrable value proposition: an agent that detects threats and executes emergency procedures (pause, revoke, migrate) faster than a human can react. It is relatively straightforward to implement using Safe's existing emergency modules and threat detection logic. The demo is compelling: simulate an exploit → agent responds → funds protected.
L03209 ### The Best Hybrid Idea
L03210 **PolicyGuard + IntentLedger.** Combine on-chain policy enforcement (PolicyGuard) with intent commitment and verification (IntentLedger). The agent commits its intent before execution, and the smart account enforces policies on-chain.
