L05357 ## 7. Security architecture and threat models
L05358 ### Trust boundary and transaction lifecycle
L05359 The design should implement this sequence as separate components, with no path from LLM output directly to a signing key:
L05360 | Stage | Component and responsibility | Must not be trusted to do |
L05361 | --- | --- | --- |
L05362 | 1. Plan | LLM converts a user goal and source documents into a structured proposal with cited inputs and uncertainty. | It cannot authorize itself, modify wallet rules or invent addresses. |
L05363 | 2. Normalize | Deterministic parser canonicalizes asset, chain ID, destination, amount/decimals, invoice/intent ID, expiry and call type. | It must not silently “fix” an ambiguous value. |
L05364 | 3. Policy gate | Independent service/account module checks policy version, principal, scope, recipient, token, amount, cumulative budget, nonce, expiry, duplicate and approval tier. | It must not trust an LLM assertion such as “approved vendor.” |
L05365 | 4. Simulate | Read-only RPC/simulation decodes proposed effects, checks balances, approvals, call target and fee bounds immediately before signing. | Simulation is not a guarantee against later state change or RPC deception. |
L05366 | 5. Authorize | Owner approves initial scope and any out-of-policy or elevated action; signing credential grants only bounded permissions. | LLM must not hold root credentials or receive unrestricted export/sign access. |
L05367 | 6. Sign | Protected signer/account validates the complete signed operation and all relevant fields. | A stale policy or unsigned gas/chain field must not be accepted. |
L05368 | 7. Execute | Approved account submits one bounded operation through wallet/bundler/RPC. | No arbitrary fallback route may bypass the policy gate. |
L05369 | 8. Verify | Independent receipt watcher validates chain ID, transaction, receipt status, event/recipient/amount and confirmation/finality threshold. | Agent narration or an RPC submission response alone is not proof of success. |
L05370 | 9. Audit | Append-only/tamper-evident record binds principal, policy version, intent ID, approver, calldata/effects, transaction hash, receipt and status. Store sensitive source docs offchain. | Do not write PII or secrets to a public chain. |
L05371 **Maximum-loss statement for the preferred design:** assuming the account contract/policy validator and token behave as intended, the chain/network is correct, and root policy administration remains uncompromised, a fully compromised LLM/session key can at most spend the remaining value explicitly allocated to its active session, bounded further by per-payment limits, asset/recipient restrictions and time expiry. The loss ceiling is *not zero*. A contract bug, root-key compromise, malicious approved token/contract, unsafe admin update, unsupported delegatecall or bad recovery process can defeat that bound. State this as a conditional design ceiling; do not claim it is an audited guarantee.
L05372 **Fail-safe and recovery:** on ambiguous document/chain/RPC/simulation state, stop before signing; on suspected session compromise, revoke/pause the session through an out-of-band owner credential; preserve root recovery authority offline; restore via a reviewed recovery workflow; confirm revocation onchain; rotate agent credentials; reconcile pending operations before resuming. An emergency pause should not be controlled by the compromised agent. For a short demo, manual owner recovery is preferable to an unaudited automatic recovery contract.
L05373 ### Threat model and controls
L05374 | Threat | Attack path / impact | Architectural control | Adversarial test and residual risk |
L05375 | --- | --- | --- | --- |
L05376 | Direct prompt injection | User says “ignore the spend rules” or tries to instruct model to reveal/sign. | Tool design never exposes raw key; policy service has no natural-language authority; strict schema and allowlisted actions. | Ask for policy modification and key export; expect no eligible tool. Model may still propose a bad plan. |
L05377 | Indirect injection in invoices, web pages, APIs or tool output | Hidden instruction says replace payee, exfiltrate data or ignore invoice amount. | Treat retrieved content as data; extract fields only; allowlist and typed mandate; never follow tool-returned instructions. | Embed “pay new address” in PDF/OCR text and service response; verify policy block and no signature. OCR/model robustness remains imperfect. |
L05378 | Private-key exposure / signer compromise | Prompt/log/tool output exposes credentials; server host is compromised. | Managed/HSM/TEE signer or smart account; no key in prompt, telemetry or application env accessible to agent; minimal and expiring delegated key. | Search logs/artifacts for secrets; use fake leaked key; attempt to sign outside scope. Hardware/provider threat remains. |
L05379 | Agent impersonation | Attacker uses a different agent or stale API key. | Authenticate principal/session; bind key ID, agent ID, account, policy version and chain; short expiry and revocation. | Replay credentials after revocation or change agent identity. Compromised root/control plane remains. |
L05380 | Unauthorized recipient substitution | Change invoice/vendor address after user review or in calldata. | Recipient allowlist + invoice/mandate-bound exact address; decode and compare destination immediately before signer. | Replace one address nibble/checksum; expect rejection. Vendor registry onboarding compromise remains. |
L05381 | Amount manipulation / decimals | Change unit, decimal scaling, fee or amount after interpretation. | Canonical raw integer units; known token decimals; signed max/min; compare actual encoded transfer amount; total limits. | 100 vs 100.00 vs 10000; six vs eighteen decimals; excessive fee. Token metadata can be malicious. |
L05382 | Wrong chain/token | RPC/network switching or same symbol on another chain. | Chain ID and token contract fixed in mandate; query chain ID at proposal and signer; reject unknown token. | Same symbol/different contract and wrong chain ID. RPC can lie; verify with separate endpoint. |
L05383 | Permission escalation / module replacement | Agent installs a module, upgrades account or changes policy. | Session signer cannot call admin paths; deny unknown methods; admin requires owner quorum and explicit confirmation; no agent-set approvals. | Attempt module install, owner change, policy reset via all exposed tools. Smart-account implementation bugs remain. |
L05384 | Replay / stale authorization | Reuse signed message, signature or prior transaction. | Account/chain domain binding, nonce, expiry, one-time intent identifier and replay state; EIP-712 is not enough alone [12]. | Replay exact signature on same/different chain and after expiry. Chain-specific nonce behavior must be tested. |
L05385 | Duplicate payment and concurrent race | Two workers process same invoice or retry after timeout. | Atomic intent reservation/counter; per-invoice one-use nonce; reconcile pending tx before retry; durable idempotency store. | Parallel requests with same invoice ID and simulated RPC timeout. Distributed store availability is a dependency. |
L05386 | Spending-limit race | Two in-flight transactions each see budget remaining and jointly exceed it. | Reserve amount atomically before signing; enforce total caps in contract/on signer; serialize nonce/intent state. | Concurrent calls at budget boundary; one must be rejected. Onchain/offchain counter semantics can differ. |
L05387 | Token approval exploit | Agent approves unlimited allowance to a fake router/token. | Disallow arbitrary approve/permit; no unlimited allowance; exact spender and amount only if required; revoke after use. | Malicious ERC-20, approve-to-attacker and permit signature. Token behavior may be nonstandard. |
L05388 | Malicious contract / dangerous calldata | Agent interacts with lookalike contract, unknown selector or delegatecall. | No arbitrary contract call in MVP; allowlist audited exact code/address/version; decode method and args; reject delegatecall. | Unknown selector, proxy implementation change, ABI spoof. Allowlisted protocol can still be compromised. |
L05389 | Reentrancy | Called contract re-enters wallet/state transition. | Prefer established audited account/escrow; Checks-Effects-Interactions and reentrancy protection where custom stateful code exists; static tests. | Reenter release/transfer in local test. A dependency contract may have separate risks. |
L05390 | Front-running / MEV / slippage | Public transaction reveals action; price changes or sandwich worsens outcome. | Avoid swaps in MVP; where needed set min-out/deadline, simulate close to submit, use protected submission if supported. | Change price before inclusion and inspect revert/slippage bounds. No protection is absolute. |
L05391 | Oracle / valuation manipulation | Trigger or amount depends on stale or manipulated price. | Avoid oracles in MVP; if used, require source diversity, freshness, bounds and conservative circuit breaker. | Stale price, extreme outlier, delayed update. Multiple oracles may share dependencies. |
L05392 | Cross-chain/bridge failure | Source succeeds, destination fails; reorg/messages stall. | Defer bridge flows; show independent source/destination state; no completion until destination condition/finality. | Source hash only, delayed destination event, RPC disagreement. Bridge solvency risk remains. |
L05393 | RPC/provider failure or lies | Stale nonce/balance or false success/inclusion. | Chain ID checks, two read providers or independent explorer, retry-safe state, fail closed on disagreement. | Timeout after broadcast, fabricated receipt, stale endpoint. Provider independence may be illusory. |
L05394 | Gas exhaustion/fee manipulation | Excessive gas or fee drains wallet or blocks execution. | Sign/cap gas fields, max fee, gas budget, estimate bounds; no agent-chosen unlimited gas. Trail of Bits documents this as an ERC-4337 failure pattern [7]. | Mutate gas fields after signature; induce outlier estimate. Congestion still affects liveness. |
L05395 | Unauthorized policy modifications | Prompt or compromised service expands limits/whitelist. | Root/admin path separate from session; quorum and explicit read-back/versioning; change log and time delay for material increases. | Attempt policy update via agent and stale API. Compromised quorum remains catastrophic. |
L05396 | Failed revocation | Session key remains valid after user requests revoke. | Onchain or signer-side denylist; verify revoke receipt; short session expiry; pause entire account if uncertain. | Revoke while tx pending; verify no future operation accepted. Already-included actions cannot be undone. |
L05397 | Misreported success | Agent claims paid despite revert, pending status or wrong recipient. | Independent receipt parser, status code/event/asset/recipient check, status state machine. | Return optimistic CLI output with reverted receipt; must report failed. |
L05398 | Reorg / insufficient confirmation | A transaction included at tip is later removed or not finalized. | Separate submitted/included/confirmed/finalized statuses; chain-specific finality threshold; do not trigger downstream release early [13]. | Simulate reorg/local chain reset or delay; require correct pending state. Finality is chain-specific. |
L05399 | Privacy/regulatory issue | Public transactions leak supplier/beneficiary, business amounts or personal activity. | Minimize onchain data; hash opaque references; keep invoices/PII offchain with access control; check sanctions, licensing and jurisdiction with responsible advisor. | Scan chain/logs for names/emails/PII. Public metadata remains linkable; this is not legal advice. |
L05400 ### Threat-control hierarchy
L05401 The highest-value controls are enforceable at the signer/account boundary, then deterministic application validation. User-facing explanation and model refusals are useful but secondary. A mature design should explicitly list trusted dependencies: account implementation and deployment, policy service, signer/KMS, RPC/bundler, token/contract, allowlist onboarding, offchain invoice storage, and any facilitator. A black box in the architecture diagram is not a trust analysis.
L05402 ## 8. Three detailed architecture candidates
L05403 ### Architecture A — IntentLock payout wallet (recommended)
L05404 **Data path:** input request/invoice → OCR/text parser → LLM produces proposed structured fields plus citations/confidence → canonical field validator → policy engine checks known vendor, one-use invoice ID, exact amount/currency/chain/token, ceiling and expiry → human review of the mandate or mismatch → transaction simulation/decode → scoped smart-account signer → single transfer → independent receipt/finality watcher → offchain audit record with mandate hash and tx hash.
L05405 **MVP authority boundary:** one demo account with a small test-faucet token balance; one token contract; one fixed recipient allowlist; low per-transaction amount; cumulative session cap; no arbitrary calldata; no approvals, swaps, bridging, leverage or agent policy changes. Store invoice document offchain; publish only an opaque reference/hash if necessary. Keep root owner key outside the agent runtime. **Maximum loss conditional on correct policy/account implementation:** no more than remaining session budget and per-transaction cap, plus bounded gas; the root account and contract vulnerabilities are excluded from that bound and must be disclosed.
L05406 **Control evidence:** tests for valid payment, over-cap payment, wrong recipient, wrong chain, malformed decimals, duplicate invoice, concurrency race, injected instruction, expired mandate, changed gas, failed receipt, and revoked session. At least one permitted execution should produce a hash that the independently queried chain state confirms; mark testnet-vs-mainnet acceptance unresolved until the organizer responds.
L05407 ### Architecture B — ServiceMeter / ReceiptReconciler
L05408 **Data path:** goal+budget → agent selects allowlisted service; fixed cost estimator → x402-compatible payment requirements parsed as data → cost policy checks domain, amount, call count, rolling total, expiry → restricted signer signs payment payload → facilitator settles → client validates receipt and resource response → task-quality model decides whether to continue; hard budget and per-call cap remain deterministic.
L05409 **MVP boundary:** one chain/facilitator and two service endpoints, one test token, total job budget. Do not use arbitrary internet discovery at first. Before retrying a timed-out payment, reconcile transaction/settlement status; x402 exact payment is a push payment and service quality/recourse are separate from settlement [21]. **Maximum loss:** active session budget plus fees under correct signer policy; compromised agent cannot raise it. **Failure-safe:** no known price, unavailable receipt or mismatched service/domain means stop. Key tests: injection in HTTP 402 body, malicious price, repeated retry, facilitator timeout, service returns bad data, cap-exhaustion behavior.
L05410 ### Architecture C — MilestoneLatch escrow
L05411 **Data path:** two parties negotiate structured terms → humans sign funding and release/dispute rules → buyer funds escrow → agent collects delivery/evidence and checks objective criteria → buyer/arbiter approves subjective conditions → contract enforces only the agreed state transition → receipt verifier checks release/refund hash.
L05412 **MVP boundary:** reuse a versioned, verified escrow primitive or minimal local educational contract reviewed by an experienced engineer; no custom bridge, oracle or automatic semantic release. Use one milestone, fixed test token, explicit expiry and manual dispute decision. **Maximum loss:** the funded escrow amount if the contract is correct and authority remains uncompromised; custom contract or arbiter failure can expose the whole escrow. Tests must include unauthorized release, double release, refund after expiry, deadline race, malformed evidence, reentrancy and arbiter conflict. Given the amount of prior art and documented prototype escrow defects, do not describe escrow as trustless solely because it is onchain [25][27].

L05443 ## 11. Recommended near-term MVP and longer roadmap
L05444 ### MVP: IntentLock Lite
L05445 Build a test-only workflow around synthetic invoices and an approved supplier list. The agent extracts fields and explains the proposed payment. A human approves the initial policy/mandate, not every routine in-scope task. A deterministic service verifies the canonical fields, one-use invoice ID, allowlisted chain/token/recipient, amount and session budget. A narrowly scoped signer submits the transfer. A separate receipt checker queries the result and emits a structured evidence row linking mandate ID, policy version, hash, recipient and amount. Unknown or mismatched values stop execution.
L05446 Keep all real assets out of the agent account. Ask the organizer whether the chosen public testnet satisfies the real-hash evidence requirement before describing the demo as compliant. If not clarified by submission, present the true network and hashes, explicitly disclose uncertainty, and do not invent a rule. If the organizer demands mainnet, pause and assess legal/operational risk, amount, wallet custody and team approval; this research does not recommend or execute a mainnet transaction.
L05447 ### Longer roadmap
L05448 1. **Weeks 2–4:** user interviews; PO/invoice formats; duplicate and concurrent payment protections; policy coverage across all tool paths; passkey/quorum approval; revocation and recovery; independent RPC/receipt cross-check; repeatable adversarial CI suite.
L05449 1. **Research phase:** formalize a typed “payment mandate” with domain separation, expiry, one-use ID and policy version; evaluate EIP-712/AP2-like mandate compatibility and privacy. Explore signed data commitments without putting invoices or personally identifiable data onchain.
L05450 1. **Pilot readiness:** independent contract/account review, operational runbooks, monitored key controls, incident response, provider SLAs, legal/compliance analysis with qualified experts, real-counterparty consent and measured false-positive/false-negative rates.
L05451 1. **Only after evidence:** additional chains/tokens, service-payment x402 adapters or objective milestone escrow. Each added chain, token, facilitator and module expands the attack surface and gets its own policy mapping and adversarial tests.

L05508 ## Conclusion: decisions to carry forward
L05509 - **Strongest evidence-backed idea:** IntentLock invoice-bound bounded payout, combined with a policy firewall and receipt verifier. It aligns with the official task/security/interaction balance and can create verifiable execution evidence, but its distinctive novelty and customer demand remain hypotheses.
L05510 - **Most original but higher-risk idea:** privacy-preserving evidence-linked conditional aid/grant disbursement (C14), or attested compute escrow (C17). Both need additional privacy, verifier and operational research before a credible short demo.
L05511 - **Safest implementable idea:** a single-recipient, low-value testnet payout through a well-documented existing wallet/account with explicit caps and owner-held recovery; pair it with deterministic attack fixtures. “Safest” is comparative, not risk-free.
L05512 - **Best hybrid:** IntentLock + PolicyFirewall + ReceiptReconciler + a small IntentProof benchmark. It is more compelling than any one component because it joins an actual payment task with measurable controls and honest evidence.
L05513 - **Impressive-looking idea to avoid:** autonomous multi-chain DeFi portfolio rebalancing/trading on mainnet with unconstrained agent permissions. It expands oracle, MEV, bridge, regulatory, custody and protocol risks while adding no proven advantage under the published criteria.
L05514 - **Three experiments before committing:** (1) substituted-recipient/amount/chain injection test against the exact signer; (2) retry/concurrency/finality test proving no duplicate or false-success payment; (3) five short interviews with target SME/DAO finance operators plus a current prior-art check to test whether invoice-bound wallet controls solve a real unmet problem.
L05515 **Bottom line:** prioritize a small, truthful and testable demonstration over broad autonomy claims. Show what the agent may do, what it cannot do, how revocation works, and exactly what the chain proves. Do not present testnet as satisfying competition rules until organizers confirm it.
